Important Exam Note: π vs 22/7

π is an Irrational Number (non-terminating & non-repeating decimal), whereas 22/7 is a Rational Number used as a convenient approximate fraction in calculations!

Definitions:

1

Real Numbers

Numbers that can be found on the number line, including both rational and irrational numbers.

Examples: -3, 0, 1/2, √5, π
2

Rational Numbers

Numbers that can be expressed as a fraction p/q of two integers, where q ≠ 0.

Examples: 2/3, -5/7, 4 (4/1), 0.5
3

Irrational Numbers

Numbers that cannot be expressed as a simple fraction. Their decimal representation is non-terminating and non-repeating.

Examples: √3, -√2, π (Pi)
4

Integers

Whole numbers and their negative counterparts. Excludes fractional or decimal values.

Examples: (..., -3, -2, -1, 0, 1, 2, 3, ...)
5

Prime Numbers

Natural numbers greater than 1 that have only two positive divisors: 1 and themselves.

Examples: (2, 3, 5, 7, 11, 13, 17, ...)
6

Composite Numbers

Natural numbers greater than 1 that have more than two positive factors.

Examples: (4, 6, 8, 9, 10, 12, 14, 15, ...)
Algebraic Laws

Properties of Real Numbers

Real numbers obey fundamental algebraic laws across addition and multiplication.

1. Closure Property

The sum or product of any two real numbers is always a real number.

a + b ∈ ℝ and a × b ∈ ℝ
2. Commutative Property

Changing the order of operands does not change the result.

a + b = b + a and ab = ba
3. Associative Property

Grouping of numbers does not alter the sum or product.

(a + b) + c = a + (b + c)
(ab)c = a(bc)
4. Distributive Property

Multiplying a sum by a number is the same as multiplying each addend individually.

a(b + c) = ab + ac
5. Identity Property

Additive identity is 0; Multiplicative identity is 1.

a + 0 = a
a × 1 = a
6. Inverse Property

Every real number has an additive inverse and multiplicative inverse.

Additive: a + (-a) = 0
Multiplicative: a × (1/a) = 1 (a ≠ 0)
