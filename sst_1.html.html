Location of India

CBSE Class 7
  • India is located in South Asia.
  • Global Position:Located in the Northern and Eastern Hemispheres (Northeast angle / ईशान कोण).
  • Latitudinal Extent: 8°4′ N to 37°6′ N (Southernmost point of territory reaches 6°45′ N).
  • Longitudinal Extent: 68°7′ E to 97°25′ E .
  • The Tropic of Cancer (23½° N) passes through 8 Indian states: Gujarat, Rajasthan, Madhya Pradesh, Chhattisgarh, Jharkhand, West Bengal, Tripura, and Mizoram.
  • The Standard Meridian of India (82½° E) passes through 5 states: Uttar Pradesh, Madhya Pradesh, Chhattisgarh, Odisha, and Andhra Pradesh. Interestingly, it intersects the Tropic of Cancer at Surguja in Chhattisgarh, making Madhya Pradesh and Chhattisgarh the only two states through which both lines pass.

  • India is the 7th largest country in the world (1. Russia --> 2. Canada --> 3. China --> 4. United States --> 5. Brazil --> 6. Australia --> 7. India 🇮🇳 --> 8. Argentina --> 9. Kazakhstan --> 10. Algeria).
  • India is surrounded by:
    • Arabian Sea in the west
    • Bay of Bengal in the east
    • Indian Ocean in the south
  • India shares land boundaries with countries such as Pakistan, Afghanistan, China, Nepal, Bhutan, Bangladesh and Myanmar.

📏 Dimensions of India

  • North-to-South Distance: 3,214 km
  • East-to-West Distance: 2,933 km
  • Distance from Equator: 876 km

📍 Important Point

  • Northernmost Point: Indira Col (PoK) (Undisputed: NJ9842 in Ladakh)
  • Southernmost Point (Overall): Indira Point / Pygmalion Point / Parsons Point (Great Nicobar)
  • Southernmost Point (Mainland): Kanyakumari (Cape Comorin)
  • Easternmost Point: Kibithu (Walangu), Arunachal Pradesh
  • Westernmost Point: Ghuar Mota (Gaur Mata), Gujarat

💡 Key Term: Kumari Shifting Cultivation

Shifting agriculture practiced in the Western Ghats is locally known as Kumari.

2

Physical Divisions of India

India has six major physical divisions:

1. The Great Mountains of the North
2. The Northern/Gangetic Plains
3. The Thar Desert
4. The Peninsular Plateau
5. The Coastal Plains
6. The Islands

These divisions differ in their landforms, climate, soil, vegetation and human activities.

3

The Great Mountains of the North 🏔️

The northern mountains are mainly formed by the Himalayas.

Main features

  • The Himalayas are young fold mountains.
  • They stretch from Jammu and Kashmir/Ladakh in the west to Arunachal Pradesh in the east.
  • They act as a natural barrier between India and the cold winds of Central Asia.
  • They are the source of many important rivers such as the Ganga, Yamuna and Brahmaputra.
  • They receive snowfall and have many glaciers.

Three parallel ranges of the Himalayas

1. Himadri

(Greater Himalayas)

  • Highest and most continuous range.
  • Many high peaks and glaciers are found here.

2. Himachal

(Lesser Himalayas)

  • Lies south of the Himadri.
  • Famous valleys and hill stations are found here.

3. Shiwaliks

(Outer Himalayas)

  • Outermost and lowest range.
  • Made mainly of loose sediments.
  • Lies to the south of Lesser Himalayas.

⭐ Importance of the Himalayas

  • Protect India from extremely cold winds.
  • Cause rainfall by stopping moisture-laden winds.
  • Provide water through rivers and glaciers.
  • Support forests and wildlife.
  • Important for tourism and agriculture.
4

Ladakh – The Cold Desert of India 🏜️❄️

  • Ladakh is a high-altitude region in northern India.
  • It is called the Cold Desert of India.
  • It lies between the Karakoram Range and the Himalayas.
  • It receives very little rainfall.
  • Winters are extremely cold.
  • The landscape contains mountains, barren land, glaciers and valleys.

Vegetation

  • Vegetation is sparse because of the cold and dry climate.
  • Grass and small shrubs grow in some areas.

People and occupations

  • People traditionally practise animal rearing and limited agriculture.
  • Animals such as yak, sheep and goats are important.
  • Tourism is also an important activity.

⭐ Why is Ladakh called a cold desert?

Answer: Because it is very cold and dry, with very little rainfall and sparse vegetation.

5

The Gangetic Plains 🌾

  • The Gangetic Plains are part of the Northern Plains of India.
  • They are formed by the deposition of alluvial soil brought by rivers.
  • Major rivers include: Ganga, Yamuna, Ghaghara, Gandak, Kosi.

Main features

  • The land is flat and fertile.
  • It is one of the most important agricultural regions of India.
  • Crops such as rice, wheat, sugarcane and pulses are grown.
  • Many large cities and settlements are located here because of fertile soil and water availability.
⭐ Important: The Northern Plains are among the most densely populated regions of India.
6

The Thar Desert 🏜️

  • The Thar Desert is mainly located in western Rajasthan.
  • It is also called the Great Indian Desert.
  • It receives very little rainfall.
  • It has sandy soil and sand dunes.
  • The climate is generally hot and dry.

Vegetation

Plants are adapted to dry conditions, such as:

  • Thorny bushes
  • Cactus-like plants
  • Grasses

People

  • People practise animal rearing and agriculture where water is available.
  • Irrigation has made farming possible in some areas.
⭐ Important: The Aravalli Range lies to the southeast of the Thar Desert.
7

The Peninsular Plateau ⛰️

  • The Peninsular Plateau is a very old landform.
  • It is made mainly of hard rocks.
  • It is roughly triangular in shape.
  • It is rich in minerals.

Two important divisions

Central Highlands

  • Located north of the Narmada River.
  • Includes areas of the Malwa Plateau.

Deccan Plateau

  • Lies south of the Narmada River.
  • It is surrounded by the Western Ghats and Eastern Ghats.

Importance

  • Rich in minerals such as iron ore, coal and manganese.
  • Important for mining and industries.
  • Many rivers flow through the plateau.
8

Coastal Plains 🌊

The coastal plains lie between the Peninsular Plateau and the sea. They are divided into:

Western Coastal Plains

  • Lie between the Western Ghats and Arabian Sea.
  • Generally narrower than the eastern coastal plains.
  • Important sections include Konkan and Malabar Coast.

Eastern Coastal Plains

  • Lie between the Eastern Ghats and Bay of Bengal.
  • Generally wider than the western coastal plains.
  • Large river deltas are found here.
  • Important rivers include Mahanadi, Godavari, Krishna and Kaveri.

⭐ Difference

Western Coastal Plains Eastern Coastal Plains
Along Arabian Sea Along Bay of Bengal
Generally narrow Generally wider
Few large deltas Large deltas are common
Konkan & Malabar Northern Circars & Coromandel
9

Islands 🏝️

India has two major groups of islands:

Andaman and Nicobar Islands

  • Located in the Bay of Bengal.
  • They consist of many islands.
  • They are known for forests and rich biodiversity.

Lakshadweep Islands

  • Located in the Arabian Sea.
  • They are mainly coral islands.
  • They are known for beautiful lagoons and beaches.
⭐ Easy trick: Andaman & Nicobar → Bay of Bengal  |  Lakshadweep → Arabian Sea
10

Aravalli Hills

  • The Aravalli Range is one of the oldest mountain ranges in India.
  • It extends mainly across Rajasthan.
  • It runs roughly from the northeast towards the southwest.
  • Guru Shikhar in the Aravallis is the highest peak.
  • The Aravallis help separate the Thar Desert from the more fertile regions to its east.
11

The North-Eastern Hills 🌳

The north-eastern region has several hills and mountain ranges. Important hills/ranges include:

Patkai Hills Naga Hills Mizo/Lushai Hills Garo Hills Khasi Hills Jaintia Hills

Main features

  • The region receives heavy rainfall.
  • It has thick forests and rich biodiversity.
  • Many different tribal communities live here.
  • The hills and valleys make the region geographically diverse.
⭐ Meghalaya Hills: The Garo, Khasi and Jaintia Hills are important hill ranges of Meghalaya.
